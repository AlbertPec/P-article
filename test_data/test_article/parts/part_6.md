## PART 6 — Model verification

### 3. Model verification

The accuracy of the calm-water resistance model and the speed-loss model directly affects the reliability of route estimates. The authors verify the calm-water resistance model using ship-model test data and main-engine test-bed data. The speed-loss model is assessed by comparison with results from previous studies.

### 3.1. Verification of the calm-water resistance model

Figures 2 and 3 compare simulated calm-water resistance with ship-model test results for the VLCC under full-load and ballast conditions.

The maximum errors are:

- full load: 1.73%;
- ballast condition: 0.20%.

Both errors are below 2%, indicating good agreement between the model and experimental results.

### 3.2. Verification of the ship speed-loss model

Because ship designs and structural parameters differ, wind and wave effects also vary between vessels. Direct experimental data on speed loss caused by weather are difficult to obtain. The authors therefore assess the speed-loss model indirectly by comparing the characteristics predicted by the Kwon method with findings from previous studies.

The Kwon method has been used in research on ore carriers, containerships and oil tankers. It is also computationally efficient and has been recommended for predicting involuntary ship speed loss.

The study examines speed loss under head-sea/headwind and bow-sea/bow-wind conditions for Beaufort numbers from 3 to 8, assuming constant main-engine power and initial speed. The predicted speed changes nonlinearly with Beaufort number. Speed loss is greater in head-sea and headwind conditions than in bow-sea and bow-wind conditions, consistent with observed ship behavior.
