#### 4.2. Route-estimation results and discussion

Figure 8 summarizes the estimated indicators for the three pre-planned routes.

![Route-estimation indicators — Figure 8](test-article/images/test-article.pdf-0007-12.png)

**Figure 8.** Estimation indicators of the pre-planned routes.

Route 1 gives the shortest ETA. Compared with Routes 2 and 3, it saves 36.58 hours and 21.08 hours, respectively. However, its fuel consumption is higher by 179.00 t and 102.17 t, and its CO₂ emissions are higher by 557.48 t and 318.21 t.

Route 2 gives the lowest fuel consumption and CO₂ emissions. Compared with Routes 1 and 3, it reduces voyage fuel consumption by 179.00 t and 76.83 t, and CO₂ emissions by 557.48 t and 239.27 t, respectively. Its disadvantage is a longer voyage distance: 329.23 nautical miles more than Route 1 and 386.52 nautical miles more than Route 3.

The comparison illustrates the trade-off between time and fuel. Under the shortest-ETA objective, saving one hour can require a substantial increase in fuel use, emissions and cost. In the case study, the additional fuel consumption associated with Route 1 is approximately 0.94 t per hour saved relative to Route 2. The corresponding increase in CO₂ emissions is approximately 2.92 t per hour saved, while the cost rises by about 18.27 USD per hour saved.

Under the minimum-fuel objective, Route 2 takes longer than Route 1 and Route 3, but it reduces fuel consumption and emissions per nautical mile. The study reports reductions in fuel consumption per nautical mile of 0.05 t/nm and 0.03 t/nm, and reductions in CO₂ emissions per nautical mile of 0.16 t/nm and 0.10 t/nm, respectively, compared with the other routes. The corresponding cost savings are also reported as substantial.

These results show that a route's geographic length alone is not a reliable indicator of voyage efficiency. Weather exposure can make a longer route more economical than a shorter one.
