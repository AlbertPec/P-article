### Route 1

Route 1 has substantially higher speeds and additional resistance than the other routes. If the ship maintains the initial speed of 16 knots, the required main-engine power on almost all sailing legs exceeds the rated power of 24,720 kW. The maximum required power reaches 25,775.76 kW.

The engine-power constraint therefore forces speed reductions on several legs. For example:

- on Leg 17, wind and waves together with the engine-power limit cause a speed reduction of 0.49 knots;
- on Leg 19, which is near a strait, navigational-safety considerations cause a reduction of 0.69 knots.

### Route 2

Route 2 has lower speed and additional resistance than the other routes. Main-engine power remains below the rated limit on every sailing leg, so the engine-power constraint does not require additional speed reduction.

The highest engine power occurs on Legs 15 and 16 and is 19,739.26 kW. On these legs, speed is 15.91 knots and additional resistance power is 2,144.34 kW.

Subject to the ETA limit, Leg 9 has the lowest speed, at 14.08 knots, excluding reductions imposed by safety factors. Although Route 2 is longer, many of its legs have following winds, crosswinds or low significant wave heights. These conditions limit the increase in additional resistance and help reduce fuel consumption.

### Route 3

Route 3 is the shortest in distance, but it does not provide the lowest fuel consumption or ETA. Its weather conditions resemble those on Route 1, with headwinds or crosswinds on multiple legs.

On Leg 14, the vessel encounters Beaufort force 7, significant wave height of 4.5 m and headwind. The synchronous rolling wavelength is 99.79 m, below the maximum-energy wavelength of 180 m. Additional resistance power reaches 2,333.18 kW and main-engine power is 22,671.89 kW. The vessel experiences a severe speed loss of 1.64 knots.

This case demonstrates that reducing route distance does not necessarily reduce fuel consumption or voyage time when weather conditions are unfavorable.

### Fuel consumption per nautical mile

Average fuel consumption per nautical mile is:

| Route | Average fuel consumption |
|---|---:|
| Route 1 | 246.86 kg/nm |
| Route 2 | 195.14 kg/nm |
| Route 3 | 227.55 kg/nm |

Route 1 has the highest fuel consumption per nautical mile, while Route 2 has the lowest. Route 3 lies between them. Higher speed and rougher weather increase resistance, which raises required engine power and ultimately increases fuel consumption and emissions.
